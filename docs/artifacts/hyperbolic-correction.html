The optimizer was the problem, not the geometry

Seven experiments concluded hyperbolic embeddings were useless for hierarchy. All seven were measuring a broken gradient descent. Replacing the optimizer with a deterministic construction reversed the result: hyperbolic represents WordNet's tree better in two dimensions than Euclidean does in eight.

Sarkar's construction, live

Children are placed at even angular intervals around each parent and pushed outward by a hyperbolic step τ, positioned through Möbius isometries. No gradient descent, no loss function — the tree is placed, not learned. Drag τ to watch the exponential volume near the boundary do the work, then keep going until float precision fails.

τ sweep — and the precision wall

Larger τ separates levels more sharply and fidelity climbs, exactly as Sala et al. predict. It also drives points toward the boundary, and past τ≈3 float64 runs out of room to distinguish them. The degradation at τ=5 is not a tuning failure; it is the documented limit of the representation.

What the failure actually looked like

Gradient descent did not fail loudly. It found a degenerate optimum: shoving every point against the ball's projection ceiling maximizes distance from negative samples, so the loss fell steadily while the representation stopped encoding anything.

Loss was never a health signal. It improved monotonically the entire time the geometry was collapsing. Nothing in the pipeline could distinguish learning from collapse — which is why the error survived seven experiments and two published conclusions.

Three corrections

Each conclusion was published, then overturned by the next check. The sequence matters: every step was a real finding at the time, defeated by a better control.

  1. Retracted — degenerate sampling

    “Hyperbolic stores hierarchy without collapse”

    Evaluation pairs were drawn at random. In a broad tree, random nodes almost always meet only at the root — 643 of 800 pairs shared one identical distance. Correlation against a near-constant target is meaningless.

  2. Withdrawn — failed control

    “Euclidean wins everywhere”

    Stratified sampling fixed the target and flipped the result. But the hyperbolic arm scored 0.391 on WordNet reconstruction where the literature reports ≈0.98 — a benchmark with a known answer, failed. The apparatus, not the geometry.

  3. Standing

    The construction wins in 2D

    Sala et al. abandon gradient descent for hyperbolic embeddings precisely because it suffers poor local minima. Substituting Sarkar's combinatorial construction produced +0.864 in two dimensions — and retroactively invalidated every hyperbolic-negative result in the project, including six clustering losses.
