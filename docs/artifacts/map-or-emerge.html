Map or Emerge — two jobs, one clustering engine
Clause & document taxonomy

Two jobs that look identical and fail differently

Both start with a pile of documents and end with structure. But mapping into a taxonomy you already have and discovering one you don't are different problems, with different failure modes and different definitions of success. Conflating them is why most tools only do one well.

The gate is the product. Cluster cohesion — computed with zero model calls — predicts which clusters are safe to trust (98.0% accurate) and which need reading (79.2%). Spend the calls only on the second kind.

The two modes

Map

“Assign these to my taxonomy.”

You already have a clause library, a playbook, a schema. The job is putting 60,000 items in the right box.

Failure mode
Wrong node. Measured against the reference.
Success metric
Accuracy / purity vs the known taxonomy.
Coarse output is
a failure — you asked for a specific box.
Measured
96.2% at 80 LLM calls, matching a 400-call exhaustive read.
Emerge

“I don't have a taxonomy. Find one.”

Nobody has written down what's in this corpus. The categories have to come out of the documents themselves.

Failure mode
False merge — two distinct concepts stuck together.
Success metric
Coherence, not agreement with a reference.
Coarse output is
not wrong — just a higher level of the tree.
Measured
False merges cut from 66% to 15.9%.

Scoring emerge against a reference taxonomy is a category error. On news articles the system merged rec.autos with rec.motorcycles, and mac.hardware with ibm.pc.hardware — arguably a better taxonomy than the official one, scored as two mistakes.

What the operator actually does

Map flow
  1. systemEmbeds every item locally, assigns to the nearest node.
  2. systemScores each cluster's cohesion — no model calls.
  3. systemTrusts the confident clusters, routes the rest to a per-item read.
  4. humanAdjudicates only the flagged cells.
Emerge flow
  1. systemClusters blind — no labels, no categories supplied.
  2. systemSplits only where a real seam exists; stops when coherent.
  3. systemNames each cluster in one short canonical term.
  4. humanRenames or merges the handful that are off.
  5. systemRe-clusters the whole corpus around the corrected names.

The human never touches the O(N) work. Naming 20 clusters organized 6,000 documents — about 300× fewer decisions than labelling them one by one. A rename isn't cosmetic: the name becomes an anchor and the corpus re-clusters around it.

Why not just search for it

On a field with a clean pattern, a regular expression silently dropped 519 of 3,167 clauses that plainly named their state — 16%, lost to capitalisation and phrasing alone. It returned nothing and reported nothing.

THIS AMENDMENT SHALL BE GOVERNED BY THE LAWS OF THE STATE OF NEW YORK…

Matched by meaning, missed by pattern. Clauses that genuinely name no jurisdiction end up in their own visible bucket instead of vanishing.
